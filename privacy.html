Privacy policy

Last updated November 23, 2026 · Applies to Sorello for iPhone (including version 0.1.0), previously named LocalFlow for iOS.

On-device processing and no uploads

Sorello performs English speech recognition on your iPhone using a speech model bundled inside the app. It does not upload your audio, transcripts, voice notes, custom dictionary, or typed text to the developer or to any transcription service. No account is required. Sorello contains no advertising, tracking, or analytics SDKs, and the app, keyboard, and Live Activity contain no code to connect to a transcription or analytics server.

When dictating directly inside the keyboard extension (while no app session is running), the keyboard uses a local, hidden WebKit audio-capture page stored inside the app with a non-persistent data store, a strict content security policy, and all external navigation blocked. Nothing you type or dictate is sent over the network.

Microphone, Face ID, and keyboard permissions

Microphone permission is needed to capture speech when you dictate or record a voice note. iOS shows its orange microphone indicator whenever the microphone is active. Sorello can activate the microphone in four situations:

  1. In-keyboard dictation (default when no session is running): When you tap Start on the keyboard, it opens microphone capture for that dictation (up to 5 minutes) and closes capture when you tap Stop, tap Cancel, hide the keyboard, or start an app session.
  2. Bluetooth microphone pre-hold (before tapping Start): By default, both Dictate in the keyboard and Use iPhone microphone are enabled. When both are on, you have granted Full Access and microphone permission, no app session or voice note is running, Bluetooth headphones are connected, and no other app is playing audio, opening the Sorello keyboard automatically opens microphone capture before you tap Start to lock audio routing to your iPhone’s built-in microphone (avoiding a route-switching delay that cuts off your first words) and keeps that route ready after a dictation stops. While held, iOS displays its orange microphone indicator and Bluetooth headphones enter hands-free mode, but no audio is processed, transcribed, or stored: the capture page does not attach audio processing until you tap Start, and any audio still in flight after Stop is discarded. To stop the hold immediately, dismiss or switch away from the Sorello keyboard. To disable pre-holding, turn off Use iPhone microphone on Sorello’s Home screen (so iOS chooses the microphone, including Bluetooth), turn off Dictate in the keyboard in Sorello › Diagnostics, or revoke microphone access or Full Access in iOS Settings.
  3. App dictation sessions and Voice Notes: When you start a session or a voice note—in Sorello, from the Live Activity, or from the Dictation Session or Voice Note controls (which require Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode when triggered from the Lock Screen or Control Center)—the app keeps the microphone ready in the background and while locked so later dictations or ongoing voice notes work without switching apps. Between dictations, incoming audio buffers are dropped unconverted in the audio callback and never retained. Sessions end when you tap End session (or toggle the Dictation Session control), when interrupted (for example, by a phone call), or after your configured idle timeout (5 minutes by default). Voice notes stop when you tap Stop (in Sorello, on the keyboard, on the Live Activity, or via the Voice Note control), end the session, or reach their safety limit (60 minutes on battery, or shortly before iOS’s 8-hour Live Activity limit when plugged in).
  4. Custom dictionary strength test: Recording a short test sentence (up to 15 seconds, foreground only) in Dictionary › Find strength processes audio in memory and discards it immediately afterward.

Face ID, Touch ID, or passcode is requested only when you set up or unlock Sorello’s optional Notes vault. Authentication is handled by iOS; Sorello never sees biometric data or your passcode.

Keyboard Full Access allows the keyboard to communicate with the Sorello app through a shared on-device container (for dictation handoff, shared settings, adding corrected words to your dictionary, and saving words you choose never to autocorrect) and enables in-keyboard dictation, Autocorrect, Soft key edges, and Typing haptics. Sorello does not use Full Access to send keystrokes, typed text, recordings, or transcripts to any server, and the keyboard cannot access your Notes vault. iOS automatically uses the system keyboard for password and other secure fields, and the app where you insert text controls how it handles that text. Surrounding text in the active field is read in memory only for spacing, cursor editing, swipe typing, autocorrect, emoji search, and noticing dictation corrections; it is not stored or transmitted.

Ephemeral dictation vs. locally saved Notes

Audio is never saved to disk. Dictation and voice-note audio is processed in memory and discarded once transcribed (or dropped immediately between dictations and during Bluetooth pre-hold). For the single most recent streamed dictation run in the app, the speech engine temporarily keeps its in-memory encoder features (up to 16 MB) and transcript for up to 90 seconds so Sorello can measure dictionary strength if you correct a word and tap Add to dictionary; this in-memory data is cleared sooner on a new recording, session end, device lock, or dictionary edit.

Keyboard dictation has no transcript history. In-keyboard dictation transcripts stay only in keyboard memory and are discarded when inserted, after 60 seconds, or when the keyboard hides. App-session transcripts pass briefly through a temporary file in the shared on-device cache (protected by iOS Complete File Protection while locked and marked excluded from backups) and are deleted on insertion, after 60 seconds, or when the app restarts or ends a session. (If neither the app nor the keyboard runs again, a temporary file may remain encrypted on disk until either next opens.)

Notes (Voice Notes) are stored locally in an encrypted vault. Notes are optional and require an iPhone passcode to create. Only transcribed and edited text (with timestamps and pin state) is saved—never audio. Notes live in Sorello’s private app container, inaccessible to the keyboard and Live Activity extensions. Every note and recorded segment is encrypted on-device with Apple CryptoKit (HPKE with X25519, HKDF-SHA256, and ChaCha20-Poly1305) to the vault’s public key, allowing a voice note to keep saving encrypted text even while the vault or your iPhone is locked. Reading, searching, or editing notes requires unlocking the vault with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode; leaving Sorello, locking your iPhone, or tapping Lock now clears unlocked notes and keys from memory.

Encryption key, backups, recovery, and export. Your vault secret is stored in the iOS Keychain restricted to this iPhone only (requires a device passcode, excluded from iCloud Keychain and device backups, and removed by iOS if you turn off your passcode). Your encrypted note files (and optional encrypted recovery wrap) are included in standard iOS and iCloud device backups so they can move to a new iPhone. Because the Keychain secret does not transfer in backups, you must generate and save a 32-character recovery code in Notes settings if you want to reopen backed-up notes on another iPhone or after turning off your passcode. Generating a new code invalidates the previous one. Sorello and Audeo Labs cannot recover a lost recovery code or unlock sealed notes. Without the device key or recovery code, the only option is Erase all notes. Sorello does not currently provide built-in file export or cloud sync for Notes; to move text elsewhere, unlock a note and copy its text manually. You can delete individual notes or use Erase all notes to remove the vault key and all notes from your iPhone.

Other local device data. Sorello stores your settings, custom dictionary (in the app’s private preferences; a word tapped via Add to dictionary passes briefly through a backup-excluded, lock-protected shared cache file deleted on receipt or after 60 seconds), words you explicitly choose Never correct for (excluded from backups; clearable in Diagnostics), recent emoji, up to 64 content-free field-layout trait hashes, and the content-free timing/memory record of the most recent in-keyboard dictation (excluded from backups; shown only in Diagnostics, with no audio, text, or app names). The Live Activity displays only session/note status, timers, and status flags on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island—never audio, transcripts, or note text. Deleting Sorello removes its local app data, subject to iOS handling of shared containers, Keychain items, and device backups.

Optional support email

Report a bug previews the recipient address and the full email draft on screen and opens your email app only when you tap Write email. The draft includes only the displayed app version and build, iOS version, generic device type (such as iPhone), and blank reproduction prompts. It attaches no recordings, transcripts, voice notes, dictionary entries, diagnostics, logs, or other files automatically. You can edit or cancel the draft, and sending requires a separate action in your email app.

If you send a support email, the developer receives your sender address and whatever information you choose to include. The email providers that carry and store the message process it under their own privacy policies. Support correspondence is used only to respond to your request and investigate the reported issue, never for advertising or tracking. You can request deletion of your support correspondence by emailing the address below.

External links and Apple services

These support and privacy pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. If you open a support or privacy-policy link, your browser connects to GitHub, which may receive standard connection details such as your IP address. To support universal links when switching from the keyboard to Sorello, iOS itself verifies the domain association file on audeolabs.com; Sorello contains no network code for this check. Apple may separately process App Store, TestFlight, or iCloud backup data according to your Apple settings and Apple’s policies.
